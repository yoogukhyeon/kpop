Korean fan culture is organised, enthusiastic and has some unwritten rules. Knowing them makes your trip smoother — and makes you a welcome guest at fan events.

## At concerts

- **Lightsticks:** most groups have an official lightstick that syncs with the show. Buy it from official merch booths or the official shop; unofficial ones may not sync.
- **Fan chants:** fandoms learn chants for each song. Groups often post official chant guides — practise a few before the show.
- **Filming:** many concerts ban photos and video. Staff do enforce it. Follow the rules announced for your show.
- **Queueing:** merch and entry queues are long and orderly. Don't cut in or hold spots for large groups of friends.

## At music show recordings

- Expect long waits and strict instructions from staff. Listen for name checks and line-ups.
- **No photos or video** during recordings unless staff explicitly allow it.
- Pre-recordings are often for one group's fan club; respect their slot even if you're there for someone else.

## At birthday cafes

- Order a drink before taking the free gifts, and take one set per order.
- Ask before photographing other fans.
- Don't move or take decorations.

See our [birthday cafe guide](/en/guides/kpop-birthday-cafe-guide) for how they work.

## Around artists

This is the most important part:

- **Don't wait for artists** at agency buildings, homes, hotels or airports.
- **Don't follow** their cars or try to find their private schedules.
- **Don't buy or share** leaked private information such as flight details.

In Korea, these behaviours are called *sasaeng* and are widely condemned — by agencies, artists and fandoms alike.

## In public places

- Keep your voice down on the subway and in residential streets.
- Don't block shop entrances or pavements for photos.
- Pick up after yourself. Fan spots stay open when neighbours stay happy.

Being a good guest is the best way to represent your fandom in Seoul.
