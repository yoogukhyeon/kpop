Standing outside your favourite group's agency is a classic part of a K-pop trip. It's a quick photo stop rather than an attraction, so the trick is to pair it with the neighbourhood around it.

## Where the big four are

| Agency | Neighbourhood | Pair it with |
|---|---|---|
| HYBE | Yongsan | Yongsan station shopping, a walk along the Han River |
| SM Entertainment | Seongsu | Seongsu's pop-up stores and cafes |
| JYP Entertainment | Gangdong | A KSPO Dome / Olympic Park concert day |
| YG Entertainment | Hapjeong (Mapo) | Hongdae birthday cafes and busking |

Agencies move offices from time to time. Before you go, check the agency's official website for its current address — and open it in Naver Map or KakaoMap rather than Google Maps.

## What you can (and can't) see

- **These are offices.** You can photograph the building from the street, but the lobby and floors are not open to visitors.
- **Some agencies run separate fan spaces** — official stores, exhibitions or cafes — that are open to the public. These change often, so check official announcements for what's open during your dates.
- **You're unlikely to see artists**, and waiting for them isn't welcome (see below).

## Etiquette that matters

Agency staff and neighbours put up with a lot of visitors. Keeping these spots fan-friendly is up to all of us:

- Take your photos and move on — don't block the entrance or the pavement.
- Don't wait for artists at the building, their homes or the airport. In Korea this is strongly associated with *sasaeng* (stalker) behaviour, and it can get fan spaces closed.
- Don't leave gifts or letters at the door unless the agency announces an official way to send them.
- Keep the noise down; these are working streets.

## Make it a half-day

Agency visits take 15–30 minutes. Our [trip planner](/en) groups each agency with the other fan spots in the same neighbourhood, so a stop at HYBE or SM becomes a proper half-day out instead of a long detour.
