There's K-pop in Seoul every week of the year. The real question is: what do *you* want to see? Here's how the year usually flows, and how to choose dates.

## Start with your bias

The single best way to pick dates is around the things that only happen once:

- **A concert or fan meeting** — announced a few weeks to a few months ahead.
- **A member's birthday** — birthday cafes open around it every year, so you can plan far in advance. Find birthdays on each [group page](/en/groups).
- **A comeback** — weeks with music show promotions mean more chances to see the group live.

## How the year usually looks

**Spring (March–May)** — Mild weather and an active comeback and concert season. Pleasant for walking between fan spots.

**Summer (June–August)** — Hot and humid, with a rainy season. Outdoor music festivals take place in and around Seoul. Plan indoor backups.

**Autumn (September–November)** — Many fans' favourite: comfortable weather, lots of concerts and outdoor events. Korea's Chuseok holiday (dates change each year) can close some shops and make transport busy.

**Winter (December–February)** — Cold, but the year-end season brings award shows and broadcasters' year-end music festivals, often with many groups on one stage. Seollal (Lunar New Year) can also affect opening hours.

Exact dates for festivals, award shows and holidays change every year — check official announcements before booking flights.

## Practical tips

- **Book accommodation early** for concert weekends; hotels near venues fill up fast.
- **Leave a buffer day** in case an event is rescheduled.
- **Weekday music shows:** staying Tuesday–Sunday gives you the most chances to attend a recording.

Once you have dates, put them in the [trip planner](/en) to see everything for your bias in one plan.
